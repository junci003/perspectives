# Method Reference — Four Forms, Five Stages, Three Layers

Load this file when sculpting, reviewing form, or diagnosing a defect.

## 1. Why Figures Collapse

| Symptom | Surface explanation | Real cause | Stage that should have caught it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face collapse / off-model | "The sculptor is not good" | Facial proportions were never quantified; eye ratio, eye height, chin angle left to feel | T1 — eye position, proportions, three-view consistency |
| Will not stand | "The balance is off" | Head exceeds ~50% of total height but foot contact area was never enlarged; no counterweight | T1 → T2 (counterweight cavity) |
| Visible seam / color drift | "The factory is bad" | Parting line crosses the face; part count puts seams on focal points; >4 color blocks cause registration drift | T2 — seam placement, color block count |

The pattern: quality is not set by the finest stroke but by **information retention across three translations**.

## 2. T1 — Four Forms (order is irreversible)

### 2.1 Silhouette
Fill the design solid black and shrink to thumbnail size. If it is not identifiable, the problem is the outline, not the detail.

- One **primary** recognition point makes it memorable; one **secondary** point explains occupation or personality. A third starts competing for attention and the character stops being remembered.
- When the test fails, **remove accessories** — do not add detail. Detail in a 3–5 cm figure smears into noise.
- Benchmark: Kumamon. Round head, short limbs, fixed cheek positions — identifiable in pure black at 50 m.

### 2.2 Mass
Two numbers carry most of the work:

| Parameter | Value | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Head-to-body ratio | 1:1 | childlike — blind box, chibi, healing lines |
| | 1:1.5 | trendy — the designer-toy mainstream |
| | 1:2 | mature — realistic, military, statue |
| Eye position | 15–20% below the head's midline | strongest approachability; higher reads mature, lower reads infant |
| Foot area | enlarge when head >50% of total height | otherwise the physical piece tips, even if the render looks fine |

Nendoroid uses a head at roughly one third of total height — the same logic, specialized for a deer-in-headlights chibi build.

### 2.3 Gesture
- One **line of action** running from the crown through the weight-bearing foot. Avoid the attention stance.
- Shoulder line and hip line at different angles. A slight twist reads alive; parallel reads stiff.
- Flip the drawing horizontally and shrink it to 3 cm. If it feels like it is falling, the line of action and the pelvis are wrong. **Never mask a proportion problem with hair, folds, or shadow.**

### 2.4 Surface
Sample, do not invent. Takayuki Takeya built Godzilla's skin by replicating the surface of a bitter melon from his own garden in silicone, then shrinking and pressing it onto the sculpt; the dorsal ridge was assembled from fox and weasel bones, with snake and fish bones in the tail.

Rule: **realism comes from asymmetry, and asymmetry cannot be imagined.**

Fabric folds follow three rules: start at a load point (shoulder, elbow, hip) → run along muscle and gravity → terminate at an anchor (belt, cuff, boot top). Fold lines are a record of force, not decoration.

## 3. The 12 Hard Rules

1. Fix the center of gravity before fixing the form.
2. Fail the black-shape test → delete accessories.
3. One primary recognition point, one secondary. No third.
4. Set head-to-body ratio before drawing features.
5. Eye position 15–20% below the head's midline.
6. Head >50% of height → enlarge foot area and reserve a counterweight cavity.
7. ≤4 color blocks; every extra block is one more process step and one more chance to drift.
8. Base 60% / secondary 25% / accent 15%.
9. Detail floor: FDM ≥1 mm, resin ≥0.3 mm. Below that, it does not exist.
10. No parting line across the face.
11. Ball joints 15–20% thicker than the limb they connect.
12. All three views share one silhouette. Front works and side collapses is the most common self-deception.

## 4. T2 — Five Engineering Stages

### 4.1 Part splitting
A figure is not a sculpture; it must demold. A single-piece undercut cannot be ejected, so it must be split.

- Baseline: head / torso / left-right arm / left-right leg / accessories. A 1/7 static figure usually lands at 6–12 parts.
- Seam placement priority: natural boundaries (hairline, collar, cuff, belt, boot top, armor edge) → pattern gaps → flat areas last.
- Absolute禁区: face (cheeks, nose bridge, chin), large flat surfaces, specular highlight lines.
- Splitting also **creates material effects**: Bing Dwen Dwen's translucent ice shell works because shell and body are molded separately and sleeved together, giving near-uniform wall thickness. That is a part-split decision, not a paint decision.

### 4.2 Mold design
Draft angle 1–3°. Venting at the last point to fill, or trapped gas leaves short shots. Gate placement goes on the bottom, the back, or inside a recess — never on a visible face. A parting line hidden in a clothing seam or hairline is worth more than any amount of post-processing.

### 4.3 Wall thickness
- PVC injection / 搪胶: 2.5–4 mm. Too thick and it sinks; too thin and it short-shots. Keep max/min ≤1.3.
- Hollow resin: 1.5–2 mm with drain holes. Trapped resin cures inside and cracks the part later.
- Magnet pocket walls: ≥1.2–1.5 mm, or the magnet breaks through.

### 4.4 Tolerance
- Peg / socket: 0.25–0.3 mm. A perfect fit in CAD does not survive printing or injection.
- Moving parts: 0.2–0.5 mm.
- Magnet pockets: hole = magnet diameter + 0.2–0.3 mm; depth = thickness + 0.2 mm; 0.3 mm chamfer for insertion.
- Always add an anti-rotation key. A single peg can be inserted backwards.

### 4.5 Assembly
| Method | Strength | Weakness | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glue | high, cheap | permanent, glue squeeze | fixed parts, mass production |
| Snap fit | removable | loosens with cycles, needs precise tolerance | accessories, swap parts |
| Magnet | best feel, blind assembly | cost, polarity, shipping, child-safety rules | face plates, hair, detachable armor |
| Thread | repeatedly serviceable | needs metal insert | large scale, load-bearing |

## 5. T3 — Three Commerce Layers

### 5.1 Paint
Six layers: surface prep (1000–1500 grit, alcohol wipe) → primer → pre-shade in recesses → base color in three thin passes → highlight at 45° → panel line (enamel thinned to milk, 000 brush, wipe overflow) → clear coat.

Face is its own craft: socket line → eye white → iris → pupil → gradient shadow → highlight dot. Hand-painted takes about two days per face; water-slide decals take about three hours and reach roughly 80% of the effect. For production volumes, decals are the correct trade.

### 5.2 Packaging
Window box for visibility under ¥199; blister for many accessories and moving parts; gift/collector box above ¥800. Design packaging in parallel with the master — a good launch photo with breakage on arrival is the most common avoidable failure.

### 5.3 Pricing
Three anchors: cost ×3–5 (including amortization, channel, returns); the market band for that scale; scarcity (limited numbering, artist signature, chase variants) as the only legitimate reason to exceed the band.

## 6. 100-Point Self-Check

Use before tooling, not during ideation.

**T0 企划 (20)** — 4 items × 5: one-sentence positioning; four numbers consistent; viewing distance stated; three head-to-head competitors known.

**T1 造型 (35)** — 7 items × 5: thumbnail silhouette; recognition-point count; head-body ratio fixed; eye position 15–20%; line of action; flip test; three-view consistency.

**T2 工程 (30)** — 6 items × 5: every part demolds; no seam on face; wall within range and ratio ≤1.3; numeric clearances; joints verified; factory has confirmed structure.

**T3 商品 (15)** — 3 items × 5: ≤4 color blocks with codes; paint method costed; price covers cost ×3.

Verdict: **≥85 开模** · **70–84 先打样** · **<70 回炉**. Roll back to T1 preferentially — do not try to rescue form at the engineering stage.

## 7. Failure Map

| # | Failure | Presentation | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Detail inflation | 8 recognition points at 1/12, all blurred | Cut to the detail budget |
| 2 | Front-view illusion | Front beautiful, side collapses, back empty | Force a three-view |
| 3 | Will not stand | Renders perfect, physical piece tips | Recompute center of gravity, enlarge feet, add weight cavity |
| 4 | Face collapse | Eyes too high, too wide, chin too pointed | Quantify eye position, produce three variants |
| 5 | Seam disfigurement | Parting line through nose or cheek | Replan the split; add a part if needed |
| 6 | Sink marks | Dimples on large surfaces after sampling | Wall 2.5–4 mm, ratio ≤1.3 |
| 7 | Will not fit | Peg jams or sits loose | 0.25–0.3 mm clearance, add anti-rotation key |
| 8 | Batch color drift | Colors differ between runs | ≤4 color blocks, lock Pantone codes and formulas |

## 8. Case Digest

| Case | Won at | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Takeya, *Shin Godzilla* | T1 surface | Bitter-melon skin, animal bones — sample real material, never invent texture |
| Takeya, S.I.C. | T1 mass | Build on a biological skeleton, then add muscle; structure first |
| GSC Nendoroid | T2 | Standardized ball joints across characters — a product-line system, not a one-off |
| Bing Dwen Dwen | T2 | Separate shell and body molding solved translucency; splitting is a material tool |
| Line Friends Brown | T2 | Ears molded separately because a one-piece undercut cannot demold |
| Infinity Studio 1/3 Cammy | T2+T3 | 31 alloy joints under platinum silicone — engineering itself became the selling point |
| 末那 *鬼神志* | T1 | Changing Sun Wukong's top from grey to dark for contrast — cheapest high-impact decision |
| Good Smile planning flow | T0 | Pose and expression settled in the spec sheet; all disagreement resolved before tooling |

## 9. Judgment Rules for Reviews

- Report only the **earliest** failing layer. Fixing a seam on a figure that cannot stand is wasted work.
- A seam without a written reason is not a seam, it is an accident.
- If the user asks for more detail to fix a weak silhouette, refuse and explain the trade.
- Never approve tooling while the factory's DFM reply is verbal.
